Where the West Begins

A little history before you go.

You're not just partying in a themed district — the Fort Worth Stockyards are the real thing, the spot where the Old West actually went to work. Here's the short version so you can sound smart at the White Elephant.

Fort Worth started in 1849 as a U.S. Army outpost on a bluff over the Trinity River, named for General William Jenkins Worth. It was the last "civilized" stop on the edge of the frontier — and locals leaned into it. When a lawyer joked in 1875 that the town was so sleepy he saw a panther asleep on Main Street, Fort Worth embraced the insult and became "Panther City" forever.

Then came the cattle. From the 1860s through the 1870s, Fort Worth was the last major provisioning stop on the Chisholm Trail before the long drive north to the Kansas railheads. Millions of longhorns tramped through town — and "Cowtown" was born. When the Texas & Pacific Railroad arrived in 1876, the cattle could ship by rail, and the Stockyards (built 1887, incorporated as the Fort Worth Stockyards Company in 1893) exploded into one of the biggest livestock markets in the country.

By the early 1900s, meatpacking giants Armour and Swift built massive plants right here, making Fort Worth a national powerhouse — "Where the West Begins." The Cowtown Coliseum went up in just 88 days in 1908 and hosted the world's first indoor rodeo in 1918 — the very building you'll sit in on the 4th. The industry faded by the 1960s, but in 1976 the district was saved as the Fort Worth Stockyards National Historic District, and in 1999 the twice-daily Fort Worth Herd cattle drive began — the only one of its kind on Earth.

🤠 Outlaw trivia In the 1880s–90s, Fort Worth's notorious red-light district Hell's Half Acre drew gamblers and gunslingers. Butch Cassidy and the Sundance Kid and their Wild Bunch hid out here and posed for the famous "Fort Worth Five" photo in 1900. Downtown's Sundance Square is named for the Sundance Kid himself.

Friday Night Headliner

Addison Kaboom Town 🎆

Top-5 fireworks show in America per the American Pyrotechnics Association. Half a million people. Held on July 3 (not July 4) so the pros work multiple shows — which means you get fireworks two nights in a row (Kaboom Friday, Dallas skyline Saturday). Fireworks go off ~9:30pm. Now that the rodeo is Saturday's 1pm matinee, there's no conflict — Friday night is all yours for this. Head to Addison early, tailgate, then loop back to the Stockyards for the honky-tonks.

📍 Addison Circle Park 🎆 ~9:30pm Fri Jul 3 🚗 30 min from Stockyards 🅿️ Plan ahead — chaos
  • 10:30 PMKat Hasty at Billy Bob's TexasLoop back to the Stockyards. World's largest honky-tonk. Texas singer-songwriter, line dancing in the main hall. Skip Addison? Just start here at 10pm instead.
  • MidnightWhite Elephant SaloonThe 1884 cowboy saloon owned by chef Tim Love. Cowboy hats nailed to the ceiling, raised wood dance floor. Live country til close.
  • Option🍺 Thirsty ArmadilloOr swap in the Thirsty Armadillo, a lively Western bar & live-music dance hall a few doors down on Exchange Ave. Great backup if Billy Bob's or the White Elephant is packed for the holiday.
