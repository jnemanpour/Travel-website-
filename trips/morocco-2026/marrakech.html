The Red City

Founded in the 1070s by the Almoravid dynasty as their imperial capital, Marrakech earned its nickname the "Red City" from the rose-hued pisé (rammed-earth) ramparts and buildings made from local clay. One of Morocco's four imperial cities, it flourished as a trans-Saharan trade and cultural hub under the Almoravids, Almohads, and Saadians. Its labyrinthine medina — founded a millennium ago — is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, anchored by the Jemaa el-Fnaa square and the towering Koutoubia minaret.

Our days here: arrival & Jemaa el-Fnaa (Day 1), medina + palaces + hammam (Day 2), gardens + Mellah + cooking class, with an optional sunrise balloon (Day 3). See the full itinerary.

Must-See Sights

Start early to beat the late-August heat

◆ Jemaa el-Fnaa

The legendary main square — a UNESCO "Masterpiece of Oral Heritage" — alive at dusk with food stalls, musicians and storytellers.

Must-see

◆ Koutoubia Mosque

The 12th-c. landmark with a 77m minaret. Non-Muslims can't enter, but the gardens and exterior are stunning at sunset.

◆ Bahia Palace

A lavish 19th-c. palace of carved cedar, zellige tilework and tranquil courtyards.

Must-see

◆ Ben Youssef Madrasa

A 16th-c. Quranic school, magnificently restored; some of the finest Islamic architecture in Morocco. (~50 MAD)

Must-see

◆ Saadian Tombs

Ornate 16th-c. royal mausoleum, sealed for centuries and rediscovered in 1917 — famed for the Hall of Twelve Columns.

◆ Jardin Majorelle & YSL

Cobalt-blue garden once owned by Yves Saint Laurent, with the Berber Museum and adjacent YSL museum. Book timed tickets ahead.

Must-see

◆ Le Jardin Secret

A restored Islamic-Andalusian riad garden in the heart of the medina, with a tower for rooftop views.

◆ El Badi Palace

Dramatic ruins of a once-opulent Saadian palace, now home to nesting storks and sunken gardens.

◆ The Souks

A maze of covered markets north of Jemaa el-Fnaa — leather, lanterns, spices and textiles. Haggling expected.

◆ Dar Si Said

A beautiful riad museum showcasing Moroccan rugs and woodcraft.

◆ Menara Gardens

Historic olive grove with a reflecting pool and pavilion framed by the Atlas Mountains.

✡️ Jewish Heritage

The Mellah of Marrakech & living Jewish life

✡ The Mellah

Morocco's oldest Jewish quarter, established 1558 beside the royal palace; once home to thousands of Jews, today a walkable historic neighborhood with spice and jewelry/rug markets.

Must-see

✡ Slat al-Azama (Lazama) Synagogue

The Mellah's synagogue open to visitors — founded in the 16th c. around a blue-tiled courtyard, with a small museum on Jewish life. Open daily ~9am–5pm (limited Shabbat/holidays), ~10 MAD.

Must-see

✡ Beth-El Synagogue

The main active synagogue in the new town (Gueliz), built in the 1950s; the center of contemporary Jewish life, holding Shabbat and holiday services.

✡ Miaara Jewish Cemetery (Beit Hayim)

Morocco's largest Jewish cemetery (16th c.) — whitewashed tombs and the graves of revered rabbis. Open Sun–Fri daytime (closed Shabbat); men cover heads.

✡ Tomb of Rabbi Pinhas Ha-Cohen

Within the Miaara cemetery; this revered tzaddik (d. 1952) draws pilgrims, with an annual hiloula in his honor.

Visiting tip: Bring a kippah/head covering for the synagogue and cemetery, and dress modestly. Hours shift around Shabbat and holidays — our Day 3 (Wed Aug 26) is a good day for the Mellah and Lazama Synagogue.

Food & Experiences

◆ Cooking Class

Learn tagine, couscous and pastilla; many classes start with a souk spice tour.

◆ Hammam & Spa

A steam, scrub and rhassoul-clay ritual — our romantic Day 2 splurge.

◆ Rooftop Dinner

Candlelit dining over the medina, often with live Andalusian music.

◆ Atlas Day Trip

Ourika Valley or Imlil for Berber villages, waterfalls and mountain tea.

Practical Tips

Medina navigation: streets are an unsigned maze — use offline maps, note landmarks, and accept that getting a little lost is part of it. A licensed guide on day one helps.

Late-August heat: highs of 38–42 °C (100–108 °F). Sightsee early and after 5pm, rest midday, carry water, choose a riad with a pool.

Haggling: expected in the souks — start around 30–40% of the asking price and keep it good-natured.

Dress: cover shoulders and knees, especially in the Mellah and at religious sites; loose breathable cotton/linen is best.
