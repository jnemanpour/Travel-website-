Stop 3 · The Spiritual Capital

Fes

The world's oldest living medieval city
Aug 29–Sep 1, 2026 · 3 nights

The Ancient Capital

Founded in 789 CE by Idris I, Fes is the oldest of Morocco's imperial cities and one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited medieval cities. Its walled old town, Fes el-Bali, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the largest car-free urban zones on earth. Home to the University of al-Qarawiyyin — founded in 859 CE and recognized as the oldest existing, continually operating university in the world — Fes has long been Morocco's spiritual and intellectual capital: a city of madrasas, artisans and endless labyrinthine alleyways.

Our days here: guided medina + tanneries + sunset (Day 7, Sun), then Jewish heritage + a pottery workshop (Day 8, Mon). We've kept the synagogue visits off Shabbat (Sat Aug 29 fell on our desert-tour drive up) — they land on Monday, when the synagogues are open. See the itinerary →

Must-See Sights

◆ Chouara Tanneries

The iconic medieval stone dye vats, still worked by hand — viewed from the surrounding leather-shop terraces. A living industry, not a museum.

Must-see

◆ Al-Qarawiyyin

The world's oldest university (859 CE). The mosque interior is closed to non-Muslims, but the restored library and courtyard glimpses are accessible.

Must-see

◆ Bou Inania Madrasa

The most spectacular 14th-c. Marinid college — stunning zellige, carved cedar and stucco; one of the few religious buildings non-Muslims may enter.

Must-see

◆ Al-Attarine Madrasa

A jewel-box 14th-c. Quranic school near the spice market, famed for exquisitely detailed tilework.

◆ Bab Boujloud (Blue Gate)

The grand 1913 gateway into the medina — blue-tiled outside, green within; the classic entry and orientation point.

◆ Nejjarine Fountain & Museum

A beautifully tiled fountain beside the Museum of Wooden Arts & Crafts, in a restored caravanserai with a rooftop café.

◆ Merenid Tombs

Ruined hilltop tombs with the best panoramic sunset view over the entire medina.

Must-see

◆ Fes el-Bali Medina

The vast labyrinth itself — the world's largest car-free medieval city, best explored slowly with a guide.

◆ Dar Batha Museum

A 19th-c. Hispano-Moorish palace of Fassi arts around a lush Andalusian garden.

◆ Royal Palace Gates

The dazzling golden brass doors framed by zellige on Place des Alaouites, at the edge of the Mellah.

✡️ Jewish Heritage

Morocco's oldest Mellah (1438) — the community predates the city itself

✡ The Mellah of Fes

Morocco's first and oldest Jewish quarter (1438), beside the royal palace — distinguished by tall houses with carved wooden balconies and ornate lattices facing the street.

Must-see

✡ Ibn Danan Synagogue

A magnificent 17th-c. synagogue (c. 1680), restored by the World Monuments Fund and a UNESCO-listed building. Open ~9am–4pm Sun–Fri, ~25 MAD.

Must-see

✡ Slat al-Fassiyine Synagogue

One of Fes's oldest synagogues (17th c.), beautifully restored and reinaugurated in 2013; visitable.

✡ Em Habanim Synagogue

A larger early-20th-c. synagogue near the cemetery, with an adjoining school — ask locally for access.

✡ Jewish Cemetery & Museum

A hillside sea of whitewashed tombs with a small memorial museum; open to visitors.

Must-see

✡ Tomb of Solica Hachuel

The most-visited grave in the cemetery — the young martyr beheaded in 1834 for refusing to convert; distinctive vase-topped roof.

Plan for Shabbat: Ibn Danan and the other synagogues close Fridays/Saturdays. Our Monday (Aug 31) is set aside for the Mellah, synagogues and cemetery. (A new Museum of Jewish Culture in Fes was built in 2023 but may not yet be open — worth checking locally.)

Food & Experiences

◆ Fassi Pastilla

The signature dish: a sweet-savory pie of poultry, almonds and cinnamon in flaky pastry dusted with sugar.

◆ Cooking Class

Half-day classes (often with a souk tour) teaching tagine, pastilla and Moroccan breads.

◆ Pottery & Zellige Workshop

Visit the Aïn Nokbi pottery cooperatives to watch artisans hand-cut the famous mosaic tilework — and make our own keepsake.

Day 8

◆ Artisan Visits

Brass, leather, weaving and woodcarving workshops; the Nejjarine and Seffarine squares are highlights.

Practical Tips

Hire a licensed guide for the medina — with 9,000+ alleys it's genuinely disorienting, and a good guide handles navigation, context and the pushy faux-guides.

Expect to get lost: GPS signal drops in the alleys — treat wandering as part of the experience and note landmarks (Bab Boujloud, Qarawiyyin) to reorient.

Tannery mint: you'll be handed a sprig of mint at the tannery terraces — hold it to your nose; the dye vats are pungent.

Late-August heat: inland Fes is very hot (35–40 °C). Start early, carry water, rest midday. Note many sites close Fridays/Shabbat.
