The Cash Plan

Bringing $3,500 USD · what it covers and what goes on a card

Bringing dollars to exchange is the right call. The driver alone wants MAD 18,700 in cash, and Moroccan ATMs typically cap around MAD 2,000–4,000 per withdrawal with a fee each time — that would have meant five or six separate trips to a machine. Walking in with dollars sidesteps the whole problem.

Practical notes

◆ Bring crisp $100 bills

Exchange counters pay the best rate for clean, new, large-denomination notes and may refuse torn, marked or older-series bills outright. Ask the bank for new ones.

◆ Don't exchange it all at once

The dirham is a closed currency — we can't legally take more than about MAD 2,000 out of the country, and converting leftovers back needs the exchange receipts. Change it in tranches and keep every receipt.

◆ Skip the airport counter for the bulk

Change just enough on arrival for the first day or two; city bureaux de change and banks generally beat airport and hotel desks. Never exchange at a hotel front desk.

◆ Split it up

$3,500 is a lot to carry. Divide it between the two of us and two bags, and use the room safe — La Mamounia, Riad Fes and Barceló all have them.

◆ No declaration needed

Both US and Moroccan rules only require declaring cash around the $10,000 mark, so $3,500 crosses without paperwork either direction.

◆ Still bring the cards

One credit card with no foreign-transaction fee for the five hotels, and a debit card as backup if the cash runs short. Tell both banks we'll be in Morocco.

Ask the driver about staged payments anyway. Paying him in two or three instalments across the trip rather than one lump at the end is normal, and it means we're not carrying the full MAD 18,700 on us from day one.
