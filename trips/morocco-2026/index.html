Good to Know

Practical essentials for late August in Morocco.

When We're Going

Late August into early September is the tail of summer. Marrakech, Fes and the Sahara are very hot (often 38–42 °C / 100–108 °F inland) — we'll sightsee early and late and rest at midday. The mountains of Chefchaouen and the Atlantic at Casablanca are our natural cool-downs.

Money & Basics

Currency is the Moroccan dirham (MAD), roughly 10 MAD ≈ $1. Cash is king in the medinas; carry small bills for taxis, tips, and the souks (haggling is expected — start around 30–40% of the asking price). Tap water: stick to bottled. Tipping (5–10%) is customary.

For Us, as a Jewish Couple

Morocco has a deep, living Jewish history, and every city on our route has heritage we can visit — the Mellahs, historic synagogues, and cemeteries. Note: synagogues and the Casablanca Jewish museum are closed on Shabbat (Saturday), so our city guides flag the right days to visit. Each city page has a dedicated Jewish Heritage section.

Dress: Lightweight, breathable, modest clothing (shoulders & knees covered) is respectful and practical in the heat — especially at religious and heritage sites. A scarf is handy for women at synagogues and mosques.
