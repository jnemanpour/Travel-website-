Casablanca

Art Deco, ocean & Morocco's living Jewish heart
Sep 3–4, 2026 · 1 night before our flight home

The Modern Face of Morocco

Casablanca is Morocco's largest city and economic capital — a sprawling Atlantic port that wears the country's most modern face. Its downtown is a treasure of 1920s–30s French Art Deco and Mauresque architecture, while the Corniche, the colossal Hassan II Mosque, and glossy malls signal a forward-looking metropolis. It's a city of commerce and cinema legend more than postcard romance — but with a single night before our early flight, it offers a satisfying taste of contemporary Morocco and the country's living Jewish heart.

One-night reality: our flight leaves CMN at 4:00 am. We're booked at Barceló Anfa on Boulevard d'Anfa, Sep 3–4 ✓ — lovely, but 30 minutes from the airport with no shuttle, so a car has to be pre-booked for around 1:30 am. We arrive from Chefchaouen mid-afternoon — enough time for the Hassan II Mosque and, if we move quickly, the Jewish museum (both close-sensitive; see below).

Must-See Sights

◆ Hassan II Mosque

The 3rd-largest mosque in the world, built partly over the Atlantic with a 210m minaret — and the only mosque in Morocco non-Muslims can enter, by guided tour. Tours Sat–Thu 9/10/11/12/15/16; ~45–60 min, ~140 dh.

Must-see

◆ The Corniche (Ain Diab)

The seaside promenade of cafés and beach clubs — a relaxed sunset stroll along the Atlantic just west of the mosque.

◆ Art Deco Downtown

The streets around Boulevard Mohammed V hold Morocco's finest concentration of Art Deco and Mauresque façades and cinemas.

◆ Mohammed V Square

The civic heart, framed by grand French colonial buildings and a large fountain.

◆ Rick's Café

An evocative restaurant-bar recreating the 1942 film's setting, with a pianist and period decor. Reservations recommended.

◆ Quartier Habous

A 1930s French-built "New Medina" blending Moroccan arches with orderly planning — good for olives, pastries and crafts.

◆ Old Medina

The compact pre-colonial walled quarter near the port — atmospheric for a short wander.

◆ Morocco Mall

One of Africa's largest malls, on the Corniche, with an aquarium — air-conditioned downtime if needed.

✡️ Jewish Heritage

Home to Morocco's largest remaining Jewish community (~2,000–3,000)

✡ Museum of Moroccan Judaism

The only Jewish museum in the Arab world (est. 1997, rededicated by King Mohammed VI in 2016) — ritual objects, costumes, jewelry and reconstructed synagogue interiors. Typically Mon–Fri ~10:00–17:00, Sun ~11:00–15:00, closed Saturdays; ~50 dh. Call ahead: +212 522 99 49 40.

Must-see

✡ Beth-El Synagogue

The community's flagship (founded 1949), famous for its Chagall-inspired stained glass and a great crystal chandelier; seats ~500. Visits arranged via the community or a guide.

Must-see

✡ Em Habanim Synagogue

A working synagogue rooted in the Em-Habanim Jewish school tradition, still anchoring Jewish education and worship.

✡ Jewish Cemetery

In the Mellah, with well-tended white markers inscribed in Hebrew, French and Spanish.

✡ Kosher Dining

The community supports kosher restaurants, bakeries and grocers — confirm current openings and Shabbat closures locally.

Timing: The museum and synagogues are closed Saturdays (Shabbat) — our arrival on Thursday Sep 3 works well. The famous Rabbi Haim Pinto pilgrimage and tomb are in Essaouira, not Casablanca, so we won't go looking for it here.

Practical Tips

4 am flight = plan the 1:30 am ride. CMN is ~30 km / 30–40 min south of the city center. We're at Barceló Anfa rather than the Nouaceur airport cluster, so there's no 24/7 hotel shuttle to fall back on — book a car through the front desk when we check in, settle the bill that evening, and don't count on hailing one at 1 am. Hassan II Mosque tour: aim for an earlier slot (9–11 am is calmest, but our Thursday afternoon 15:00/16:00 tours work too). Dress code enforced: covered shoulders, below the knee, no shorts/sleeveless.

Shabbat: the Jewish museum and synagogues are inaccessible on Saturday — fortunately we're here on Thursday.
